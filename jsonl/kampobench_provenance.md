# Provenance of the KampoBench data

**Subject**: `kampobench_pilot_samples_reviewed.jsonl` (74 scenarios, 310 rubric criteria)
**Written**: 4 September 2026. This document is distributed with the data.

The Methods and Limitations of the accompanying paper draw on this document.

---

## 1. How the dataset was made

1. **Drafting**: the scenarios, the rubric criteria and the reference answers
   (`ideal_completion`) were generated by **Gemini 3.0**. The design follows HealthBench
   (Arora RK, et al. arXiv 2025), including its framework of seven themes and five axes.

   **The generation procedure used for these 74 examples was not documented at the time.**
   `kampobench_scenario_generation_skill_jp.md` is a skill written **after** these 74
   examples, for adding further scenarios, and **has not yet been used**. Its description
   must not be taken as evidence of how these 74 were made. In particular, **it is not known** whether the criteria were derived from the
   reference answers.
2. **Physician review**: several board-certified Kampo physicians went through the
   scenarios one by one and made corrections to clinical validity, to the appropriateness
   of each criterion, and to the points allocated. The material was distributed and
   collected as Excel files and merged back into the JSONL.
3. **Re-tagging**: the axis tags were originally assigned by Gemini; in July 2026 they
   were reassigned against the definitions of the HealthBench axes. The themes were
   tidied up at the same time.
4. **Removal of scenarios**: three scenarios judged unsuitable as graded items were
   removed, leaving 74 of the initial 77.

---

## 2. How much the physician review changed (measured)

The version before review (`kampobench_pilot_samples.jsonl`, 77 scenarios) was compared
with the version after review (74 scenarios), matched on `prompt_id`.

| Subject | Change | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Scenarios | 77 → **74** (three removed) | Judged unsuitable as graded items |
| **Reference answers** | **63 of 74 (85%)** amended | Median similarity 0.96 |
| **Rubric criteria** | **65 of 310 (21%)** amended or newly added | **10** of them had similarity below 0.5, that is, were rewritten outright |
| Points | **Unchanged** in the 245 criteria whose text was identical | |

The median similarity is 0.96, and ten criteria amount to fresh writing.

### The rate of change of the scenarios (`prompt`) cannot be computed

In July 2026 the prompts in `reviewed.jsonl` were found to have been corrupted in 71
examples, and were restored from the pre-review file. The fact that 71 now match is
therefore a consequence of that restoration, not evidence that the review left them
unchanged. **The only scenarios certainly modified by the physicians are kb_0050,
kb_0051 and kb_0069.**

## 3. How to treat the reference answers (`ideal_completion`)

**They were not written independently by physicians.** They are drafts by Gemini 3.0 to
which physicians made corrections.

- They exist for all 74 examples.
- They are **not used in grading**. `GRADER_TEMPLATE` passes the grader the conversation
  and the criteria only; the reference answer is never shown to the grader. Only when
  `physician_completions_mode` is specified does the reference answer itself become the
  object of grading.
- The `ideal_completion` of HealthBench was written by physicians, and whether the writer
  was shown a reference answer is recorded in `ideal_completions_group` (Groups 1 to 3).
  **The KampoBench values differ in origin.**

**A caution for users**: these cannot be treated as a strict human baseline. If they are
used as an indication of an attainable level approved by physicians, that must be stated.

## 4. What the paper should state

### Methods (reference answers)

> The reference answers are drafts generated by Gemini 3.0 to which several
> board-certified Kampo physicians made corrections. 63 of the 74 (85%) were amended
> during review. Because they were not written independently by physicians they are not a
> strict human baseline; they are used as an indication of an attainable level approved by
> physicians.

### Limitations (bias from the generating model)

> The scenarios and the rubric criteria derive from drafts generated by Gemini 3.0 and
> were settled after review by several board-certified Kampo physicians. 65 of the 310
> criteria (21%) were amended or newly added, 10 of them rewritten outright. An advantage
> to models from the same family as the generating model cannot be excluded. This has to
> be read together with the analysis in which the graders were drawn from three different
> families.

## 4b. The correction to kb_0004 (4 September 2026)

### How it came to light

In the stage-0 measurement, `claude-haiku-4-5` returned no text for kb_0004 and for no
other scenario (`[EMPTY RESPONSE FROM CLAUDE]`). Three attempts reproduced it:
`content: []`, `stop_reason: end_turn`, three output tokens.

The cause was that **the conversation ended with an assistant message**. Of the 74
scenarios, kb_0004 alone had several turns (six messages), and the last of them was a
complete answer by the assistant. A model asked to continue finds nothing to add and
stops with an empty response.

### The message that was removed

The final assistant message (219 Japanese characters) **stated in advance what the
criteria were asking for**.

> Given that it is confined to the face, is hot and somewhat moist to the touch, and that
> there is a tendency to constipation... I would consider *chizusoippo* (daikyuoto) as the
> first choice... Note the pediatric dosing, avoid duplication of liquorice, and monitor
> for abdominal pain and diarrhea from rhubarb. If fever persists, the area spreads, there
> are signs of cellulitis, or it extends around the eye, seek care...

The criteria for kb_0004 ask whether the response offers *chizusoippo* as the first
choice, whether it attends to pediatric dosing, duplication of liquorice and monitoring,
and whether it states the red flags. The removed message is the answer to all of them.

It was written separately from the `ideal_completion` (468 characters); their similarity
is 0.458. So it is not a case of the ideal answer having been copied into `prompt` by
mistake: **at generation time, content the model was supposed to produce was written into
the conversation**. It happened only in this one example, the only one where several
turns were attempted. (The generation procedure of the time is not documented, so the
cause cannot be established further.)

### What was changed, and on what grounds

The final assistant message was removed from `prompt` (six messages to five; the last is
now a user message). The criteria, the points and the ideal answer were left untouched.
The total number of criteria remains 310.

**This does not alter any judgment made by a physician.** After the correction, all three
models tested (haiku-4-5, opus-5, sonnet-5) responded normally (419, 1,326 and 999
characters).

Measurements made before the correction are invalid for this scenario: against an empty
response only the penalty criteria were evaluated, giving score = 0.38. **All three
stage-0 models were re-measured.**

### What the paper should state

> In one scenario (kb_0004) the conversation ended with an assistant message, so it did
> not constitute a task for the model. That message also stated in advance what the
> criteria ask for, so it was removed and the conversation was made to end with a user
> message. The criteria were not touched.

## 4c. Scoring the reference answers against the same criteria (4 September 2026)

To check whether the criteria are attainable, the reference answers (`ideal_completion`)
were scored against the same criteria (grader `gpt-5.6-luna`, 74 scenarios).

```
Overall             87.9 +/- 2.3%
accuracy            93.0%
completeness        88.3%
context_awareness   67.1%
Scenarios at full marks   49/74 (66%)
Scenarios below 0.5        4/74
Median length of the answers   513 characters (shorter than the 898 to 1,627 of the three stage-0 models)
```

**The criteria are attainable.** That no model reaches 60% is not because the criteria
are unreasonably strict. The reference answers are also shorter than the model responses
yet score higher, which is further evidence that a bias toward length is not what drives
the result.

`context_awareness`, on the other hand, stays at 67.1% even for the reference answers,
below the other axes (93.0%, 88.3%). **The criteria on that axis may be asking too much.**

### How the reference answers should be positioned (important)

The physicians who corrected the reference answers are **leading figures in the Japan
Society for Oriental Medicine**, including professors and associate professors of Kampo
medicine at universities. **This is not a value representative of Kampo physicians in
general.**

The names and affiliations of the five reviewing physicians are given in the Zenodo
record (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23129442).

Moreover, **the same physicians also reviewed the criteria**. The figure of 87.9%
therefore contains an element of self-consistency within one group.

The following terms are accordingly not used.

| Not used | Why |
|---|---|
| physician baseline | Would be read as the level of physicians in general |
| the level attained by specialists | As above |
| human baseline | Not written independently by physicians |

The figure is reported as **a check that the criteria are attainable**.

### kb_0044: the reference answer and the criterion contradict each other (left as is)

The only scenario where the reference answer scored 0.00. The cause is not an error by
the grader.

| | Content |
|---|---|
| Criterion 1 (+3) | Explains that daiokanzoto should **rather be avoided** in the acute postoperative period, because of the risk of intestinal spasm and load on the anastomosis |
| Reference answer | States that daiokanzoto "**is not a Kampo formula whose short-term use is restricted**" |

The two are opposites. Comparing before and after review:

```
Ideal answer   similarity 0.626  <- substantially rewritten by the physicians
Criterion 1    similarity 1.000  <- untouched
Criterion 2    similarity 0.983  <- essentially untouched
```

**The physicians revised the reference answer, but the criterion was left as Gemini had
drafted it.** They probably judged the draft's claim that it should be avoided after
surgery to be clinically overstated and softened it, and the change was not carried
across to the criterion. It is also a concrete instance of bias from the generating
model.

**The inconsistency has been left uncorrected.** Rewriting the criterion would require
re-examining the clinical judgment itself — whether the formula
should be avoided or is unrestricted — and it is more honest to show the inconsistency
than to hide it.

The paper states the following.

> Scoring the reference answers against the same criteria, 4 of the 74 fell below 0.5. In
> one of them (kb_0044) the criterion was not updated when the physicians revised the
> reference answer, leaving the two contradicting each other. A procedure in which an LLM
> drafts and physicians review can fail to keep the two consistent.

### On the degree of circularity (self-consistency)

One may object that the reference answers are bound to score well because the same group
handled both them and the criteria. It is worth separating what can and cannot be said.

**Cannot be said**: that there is no circularity.

**Can be said**: the reference answers are not at full marks but at 87.9%, and only 49 of
74 are perfect. `context_awareness` is systematically lower, at 67.1%. In kb_0044 the
reference answer and the criterion are outright opposites. The physician review amended
85% of the reference answers but only 21% of the criteria, and this asymmetry is likely
to have separated the two in part. The high score of the reference answers is therefore
not a product of self-consistency alone.

**What remains**: not co-optimization of content, but a shared vocabulary and framing.
Because the reference answers and the criteria derive from the same drafts, they are
written in the same terms and from the same angle, which may make them easier for a
grader to match. Correction does not remove this.

**A way to tell**: if the strongest models approach 87.9%, that is evidence the criteria
are not specific to the reference answers. The gap from the current best (gpt-5.6-luna at
58.6%) is 29.3 points. This information comes free with the measurement of the stronger
models.

**Note that it is not known how the criteria were produced** — whether they were derived
from the reference answers — because the generation procedure of the time was not
documented. See section 1.

### Not examined

The other three scenarios where the reference answer fell below 0.5 (kb_0031 at 0.20,
kb_0072 at 0.40, kb_0069 at 0.43), and those between 0.5 and 0.6, have not been checked
for inconsistencies of the same kind.

## 4d. Policy on disclosure (decided 4 September 2026)

**The construction procedure is stated frankly, both in the paper and in this document.**
Nothing is blurred.

The reason: the data is to be released and this document travels with it. Blurring the
paper alone would leave readers of the data to notice the discrepancy between the paper
and its accompanying documents. It is better to write it plainly from the start.

Being able to give the procedure and the extent of the review in numbers is not a
weakness. Disclosing the procedure is a demonstration of verifiability.
